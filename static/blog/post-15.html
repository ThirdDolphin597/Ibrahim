Intro

At the end of my last post, I said I wanted to spend more time in the machine shop and make a small ring project. I really enjoy being in the shop and creating things, so over the last two weeks, I started doing that and learned how to use both the lathe and the hybrid mill.

I made two aluminum rings on the lathe and a Turner's cube on the mill. I also got to learn how the machines work for future projects.

Learning the Lathe

I started by learning the controls on the lathe and how to set up the machine. I practiced running it at different spindle speeds and using different cutting tools. I learned how different speeds are used for different finishes and materials.

For practice, I made two separate rings. Repeating the project gave me more time to become comfortable with the controls and the order of the machining process. It also helped me see how each decision on the machine affects the final shape and finish of the part.

Programming the Hybrid Mill

After working on the lathe, I moved to the hybrid mill. I first machined the material into a cube. Starting with the basic shape helped me learn how to position the material and use the mill before moving on to the programmed cuts.

Once the cube was ready, I used the drawing below to program the mill to cut circular features into it. The drawing gave me the dimensions and depths for the two different circular cuts. I repeated those programmed cuts on each side of the cube to create the Turner's cube geometry. This was my first time going from a basic block of material to a part that depended on both the physical machine setup and a programmed toolpath.

My teacher walked me through adding the program to the mill and starting it. The process went smoothly, and I was able to watch the programmed cuts take shape.
