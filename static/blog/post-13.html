Intro

I started biking more recently, so I wanted to make a few small biking projects to get warmed up for the year. These are not huge projects, but they are useful things that give me a reason to keep practicing CAD, 3D printing, and quick prototyping.

This week I worked on two small bike parts: a display stand for an old rear shock that does not work anymore, and a mount for a bike tool pouch that I want to move between all my bikes.

Trying Fusion Assistant

I also tried using Fusion Assistant to see what it is capable of. It is supposed to help with text-to-CAD, and I had seen Autodesk show other AI features connected to Microsoft-backed tools, including image generation and turning design work into presentation material. Autodesk's education page lists Fusion as available for students, but some cloud-based services and newer AI tools can depend on the account or license. On my student account, the AI features I wanted were not available, so I could not test everything.

The text-to-CAD tool also did not work well for me. The model it made was very deformed, so it was not really useful as a starting point. It was interesting to try, but for these parts it made more sense to just model everything normally.

Testing Fusion Assistant to see how useful it would be for quick CAD ideas.

Rear Shock Display Stand

The first bike project is for a rear shock that does not work anymore. I still think it looks cool, so instead of leaving it in a drawer I decided to make a display stand for it.

Display stand model for the broken rear shock.

The stand is designed to hold the shock upright and make it look like a finished display piece. I still need to 3D print it and test the fit. Once it is printed, I will check if the shock sits at the right angle and if the stand is stable enough.

Transferable Bike Tool Pouch

The second project is a mount for a bike tool pouch. I have a tool pouch inside one of my bikes, but I want it to be transferable to all my bikes. The idea is to keep it full of tools and move it to whichever bike I am taking.

The mount attaches to the bike and uses Velcro loops to hold the tube or pouch in place. I modeled the part with slots for the Velcro so it can stay secure but still be easy to remove.

Tool pouch mount model with slots for Velcro straps.

I printed a small section first to test it on my bike before printing the full part. That helped me check the fit without wasting too much material. The next step is to print the complete mount and test if it holds the pouch securely while riding.

Conclusion

These projects are small, but they are a good way to get back into designing and printing parts. Next I need to 3D print the rear shock stand, finish the full tool pouch mount, and test both parts in real life. Once I know what works and what needs to change, I can make the next version.
